*Crypto wallets* are didital tools designed to store your private [[keys]].
They allow you to manage your cryptocurrencies and [[NFT|NFTs]] on various [[blockchain|blockchains]].

Unlike normal wallets, crypto wallets technically don’t store your crypto. Your holdings live on the [[blockchain]] and can only be accessed using a private key.

Crypto wallets are divided ino *custodial* and *non-custodial* (known as *self-custodial*) depending on who controls the private keys:

- 🔐 With *custodial* wallets, a third party like an exchange take a full control over your private keys.
- 🛡️ With *non-custodial* wallets, you have full control over your keys and the security of your assets.


![[e12a99c64b48d163ee5e92a3e9e604060a11d927-1116x1395.webp]]

Self-custodial solutions in turn are divided into *hardware* and *software* wallets.

## 🧊 Hardware wallets

- A physical device separated from an internet connection
- Operates offline
- Examples: [Ledger Nano X](https://shop.ledger.com/products/ledger-nano-x/onyx-black), [Trezor Safe 3](https://trezor.io/trezor-safe-3)

## 🔥 Software wallets

- A desktop/mobile app
- Relies on internet connection
- Examples: [MetaMask](https://metamask.io/), [OKX Wallet](https://web3.okx.com/), [Rabby Wallet](https://rabby.io/)
