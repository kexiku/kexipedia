> [!quote] ❔ Question
> Why drink milk made for cows when there is actually such a thing as human milk? Seems like the milk made by humans would actually be better for humans. I dunno. Don’t get all weird and creepy. That’s not what this is.

## 💬 Answer

This idea sounds perfectly reasonable - right up until we turn to a scientist’s best friend: **Statistics herself**.

As we all know, women make up roughly half of our population, which means they are only capable to provide milk for themselves - or sacrifice it to men.
Either way, one person out of every two is doomed to suffer from dairy deprivation.

For this very reason, milk remained a luxury item for centuries. That is precisely why we say *"don't cry over spilled milk"*: even a single drop of this nutritious beverage could sustain an average middle-class family in ancient Greece for an entire month.

Throughout history, humanity, led primarily by men, was forced to make do with desperately meager supplies of this essential substance.
The consequences were catastrophic: gender discrimination, wars, genocides, and widespread constipation caused by eating dry cereal.

Determined to put an end to this madness once and for all, Swiss biologist **Heinrich vom Eisholen** invented the cow in 1901 by crossbreeding a Bernese Big-Uddered Mare with an ordinary Dalmatian.

The result exceeded all expectations.
The brilliant scientist’s creation not only stopped the endless feud between nations, but also gave us butter, yogurt, cheesecakes - and the delicious Swiss chocolate without which no modern Weihnachtssocke would be complete.

Speaking of chocolate: a century later, Heinrich’s great-great-grandson, **Friedrich Krummhorner**, managed to breed a strain of choco-lacting cows (*taurus socolatas*).
In 2022, he achieved this by mating his elderly cow Hilda with one of Saint Nicholas' reindeer, while the latter was serving a sentence for market manipulation, toy dumping, and violations of COVID restrictions.

Thanks to the efforts of UNICEF and devoted volunteers, the first batch of chocolate cows was immediately shipped to Burundi to combat pandemic-induced famine.
Millions of African children were given the opportunity to enjoy chocolate milk instead of water from the earliest days of their lives. Lucky bastards!

Freed from the burden of breastfeeding, women began increasingly turning their attention toward careers, particularly in **bovine studies**.
According to the latest data from the US Department of Education, more than 67% of this year’s applicants have chosen 'Dairy Engineering' as their major, while the National Institute of Cheesemaking in Houston has ranked among the country’s leading educational institutions for several consecutive years.

And so, a simple act of scientific curiosity marked the beginning of a new era: the **post-dairy society**.
Neural milkers, quantum pasteurization, and cloud cowputing have long left the realm of science fiction to become an integral part of our daily lives (or, better said, *dairy* lives).

Today, only dusty chronicles and the crumbling ruins of ancient women farms preserve the memory of those barbaric pre-bovine times - the shameful stain that humanity is still striving to wash off its cowboy hats.

Now, ask yourself: is human milk *really* as beneficial as it seems? How much have we truly lost by abandoning it - and have we, perhaps, gained something far greater than mere vitamins and calcium?..
