The *bit depth* is the number of bits that are used to represent each [[Sample rate|sample]] of a digital audio.

It defines the [[dynamic range]] of an audio signal and determines how many discrete values are available to measure the height (loudness) of a sound wave at any given moment.

Each bit represents approximately 6 dB of dynamic range, so 16-bits provide 96 dB of dynamic range, and 24-bits provides 144 dB of dynamic range.

## 🔈 Common bit depths

### 16-bit

- The standard quality for audio CDs and general music streaming
- Offers 65,536 ($2^{16}$) possible amplitude values and a dynamic range of about 96 dB

### 24-bit

- The industry standard for professional audio production
- Offers over 16.7 million ($2^{24}$) possible values and a dynamic range of about 144 dB

### 32-bit

- Used primarily in internal software processing to provide massive dynamic range (over 1,500 dB) and prevent digital clipping if a recording hits peak volume
