The *sample rate* is the number of times per second an analog audio waveform is measured (sampled) and converted into a digital signal.

It's measured in Hertz (Hz) or kilohertz (kHz), where 1 kHz equals 1,000 samples per second.

Under the *Nyquist theorem*, the highest audio frequency you can record is half of the sampling rate frequency.
Since human hearing caps out at around 20 kHz, a sample rate must be at least 40 kHz to capture the full audible spectrum.

## 🎚️ Common sample rates

### 44.1 kHz

- The standard for audio CDs and general music streaming
- Covers frequencies up to about 22.05 kHz, which is just above human hearing

### 48 kHz

- The standard for video, television, and multimedia production
- Syncs well with video frames

### 96+ kHz

- Used for high-resolution audio mastering and professional recording where extra headroom is desired for high frequencies
